# Global hotspots of microbiota for lake ecological conservation
The scripts used in the study

Microbes represent the largest portion of the biodiversity in lakes and provide key services such as clean water and pathogen control for humankind. The fate of this unique biodiversity remains largely unknown in the context of global change. Here, we integrate 1,618 upper-water-column metagenomic lake samples with machine-learning models to systematically evaluate the extent to which the diversity and functional potential of lake microbiomes (archaea, bacteria, fungi, and viruses) are affected by global change. This knowledge is key to determining the candidate vulnerability zones for conservation screening of lake microbiota and functions. Our work reveals that conserving lake microbial diversity and function is challenging, as hotspots for different ecological dimensions (alpha diversity, community dissimilarity, and functional potential) are found across different lakes worldwide. Under SSP5–8.5, 61.8% of environmentally supported lakes were projected to experience moderate or strong declines in at least one ecological dimension (i.e., falling within either of the two upper decline classes in our data-driven classification). We identified 39,359 lakes as candidate microbial vulnerability zones and reported that only 13.7% of such lakes overlapped with existing protected areas. Our work provides a broad-scale screening framework for identifying candidate lake microbial vulnerability zones, while highlighting regions that warrant additional sampling, site-specific assessment and locally informed management.
